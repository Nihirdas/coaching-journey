The starting point

Where I began

I had just stepped up from many years as an individual contributor into leading several teams at once. I was strong on delivery and on structure. What I was not yet strong on was leading through people rather than through logic.

Hard conversations made me uncomfortable. My instinct was to solve a problem myself rather than to lead someone else through it. And under pressure, my frustration could show before my words did.

I knew the technical craft. I was still learning the human one.

What changed

What the coaching gave me

Frameworks that stayed with me because each one arrived tied to a real situation I was living.

01

Feedback that works

Ask permission first, speak in the “I” form, be concrete, and check my own inner stance before I begin — because most of what lands is unspoken.

02

Trust before technique

Nothing lands unless the person already trusts me. Beneath every behaviour sits an iceberg of needs and fears I have to understand first.

03

Leading with their value

I learned to open with what matters to the other person, not with my own pain. It changed how every difficult conversation went.

04

Influence, not instruction

The real shift, given my nature, was learning to influence so that an idea becomes the other person’s own.

05

Situational leadership

Meet people where they are. Give direction when something is new, then step back as they grow.

06

Name it to tame it

When a reaction rises, I notice it, decide not to act on it in the moment, and choose my response deliberately. This is the one I reach for most.

07

The weight of words

And instead of but. When instead of if. Never the words never and always.

The impact, in real life

How it showed up in my work

Not theory — the coaching changed real situations, with real people.

A tense conflict between two teams, which I reframed around shared value rather than blame — and which turned collaborative.

A disengaged team member, whom I influenced so gently that a new working habit became his own idea rather than my instruction.

A sensitive situation with a valued team member considering a move — handled through trust and honest care rather than authority.

A tool I built to make our work visible, which leadership then wanted taken wider across the organisation.
