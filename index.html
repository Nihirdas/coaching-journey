A Leadership Journey

How I coached a QA manager in fintech — over ten sessions — from strong-on-delivery to leading through people.

The story
The starting point

Where he began

When he first came to me, he had just stepped up from years as a respected individual contributor into leading three teams at once — around fourteen people in a global fintech. He was strong on delivery and on structure. What he had not yet grown into was leading through people rather than through logic.

Hard conversations made him uncomfortable. His instinct was to solve a problem himself rather than lead someone else through it — and under pressure, his frustration could surface before his words did. His profile told the story: a builder and a stabiliser, lower on influence, learning to inspire.

He knew the technical craft. Together, we set out to build the human one.

How we worked

The arc of ten sessions

The journey of a thousand miles begins with one step.
1

Foundations

We began with self-awareness — his behavioural profile, his values, and how his wiring shapes the way he leads.

2

Influence & trust

I brought the foundation every team rests on: trust — and we separated stated agreement from genuine commitment.

3

Mid-point review

We held his progress up to the light, naming honestly what was landing and what still needed work.

4

Motivation & culture

We reframed motivation: people are moved differently than you are. Leadership is giving each person what they need — not what you need.

5

Push versus pull

We mapped his team by skill and interest, and worked on leading — not only pleasing.

6

Review & close

A full reflection on how far he had come — and where the journey continues.

…and the journey continues.

My approach

How I coach

Frameworks land only when they are tied to a real situation you are living that week. That is how I work — and it is why it stays with you.

01

Real situations, never theory

Every session starts from a live moment — a meeting that went sideways, a person who went quiet. Frameworks in the abstract don’t stick; frameworks tied to a real problem do.

02

Questions that hold up a mirror

I ask the question beneath the question — often the one you are avoiding — and when a pattern shows up in the room, I name it gently, in real time.

03

Trust before technique

Nothing lands unless the person trusts you first. Beneath every behaviour sits an iceberg of needs and fears — we start there.

04

Feedback that works

Ask permission, speak in the “I” form, be concrete, and check your inner stance before you begin — because more than ninety percent of what lands is unspoken.

05

Situational leadership

Meet people where they are. Give direction when something is new, then step back as they grow.

06

The weight of words

And instead of but. When instead of if. And never the words never and always.

The results

What changed in his work

The proof of coaching is not in the room — it is in what changes on Monday morning.

A tense stand-off between two teams — reframed around shared value instead of blame — turned collaborative.

A disengaged team member, influenced so gently that a new working habit became his own idea rather than an instruction.

A valued team member weighing an exit — held through trust and honest care, not authority.

A tool he built to make his team’s work visible, which leadership then wanted taken wider across the organisation.

By the close, structure showed up not only in his tools but in his behaviour — and he had begun to lead by example.
